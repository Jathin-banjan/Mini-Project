ABSTRACT
India possesses a rich and diverse tapestry of indigenous art forms, folk paintings, and handloom textile traditions. Among these, Warli tribal art from Maharashtra, Madhubani folk paintings from Bihar, and traditional Indian textile motifs (incorporating Paisley and Bandhani patterns) reflect centuries of cultural heritage and traditional knowledge. However, rapid globalization, lack of formal digitization, and subjective manual cataloging present critical risks of cultural attrition, misattribution, and loss of provenance. This mini-project report presents HeritageAI, a research-grade, locally deployable artificial intelligence platform designed for the automatic identification, classification, explainability, and digital preservation of Indian cultural patterns. The core computer vision pipeline utilizes a customized Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) architecture engineered for three distinct pattern categories: Warli Art Motifs, Madhubani Paintings, and Indian Textile Patterns. The image processing methodology enforces rigorous aspect-ratio preserving resizing with zero-padding (letterboxing) to 224 × 224 × 3 RGB and pixel intensity normalization to [0.0, 1.0]. The deep learning architecture incorporates stacked Conv2D feature extraction layers, 2 × 2 max-pooling, dense fully connected layers with ReLU activations, dropout regularization (rate 0.45) to prevent overfitting, and a three-unit Softmax classification layer trained via the Adam optimizer (learning rate 0.0001) under Sparse Categorical Crossentropy loss. To foster algorithmic transparency, the system implements Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM) to generate spatial attention heatmaps illustrating salient feature regions that influence model predictions. The software architecture integrates a React 18 / Vite / TypeScript single-page application, an enterprise Java 21 / Spring Boot 3 RESTful microservice with JWT authentication and Flyway database migrations, and a Python 3.11 / FastAPI deep learning inference service supported by PostgreSQL. Experimental evaluations demonstrated high classification accuracy alongside low inference latency, validating the system as an effective preservation instrument for archivists, museum curators, and cultural researchers.

CHAPTER 1
INTRODUCTION

Cultural heritage embodies the historical memory, spiritual philosophy, and aesthetic identity of human civilization. In the Indian subcontinent, traditional art and handloom forms have flourished over millennia, serving not merely as decorative expressions but as visual languages reflecting tribal folklore, sacred geometry, and socio-ecological connections. Prominent among these indigenous visual expressions are:

1. Warli Art: Originating from the Sahyadri tribal communities of Maharashtra, Warli art is distinguished by rudimentary geometric elements—circles representing celestial bodies, triangles depicting sacred mountains, and stylized human stick figures engaged in ritual Tarpa circle dances.
2. Madhubani Paintings: Practiced across the Mithila region of Bihar, Madhubani folk art is renowned for prominent double outlines, parallel line hatchings, vibrant natural pigments, and non-empty backgrounds embellished with sacred fish, peacocks, and floral motifs.
3. Indian Textile Patterns: Spanning pan-Indian block printing and tie-dye traditions, incorporating iconic Paisley (Kalka) silhouettes, Bandhani geometric dot matrices, and rhythmic repeating borders.
1.1 Problem Definition

Traditional cataloging of cultural heritage artifacts is predominantly manual, labor-intensive, and reliant upon the subjective expertise of specialized art historians. Archival institutions and museums often lack automated computational tools capable of accurately distinguishing between visual traditions that share overlapping geometric primitives, ingesting arbitrary digital photographs while mitigating distortions introduced by non-uniform aspect ratios and lighting, explaining the internal rationale of black-box deep neural networks to domain historians, and decoupling provisional machine-generated predictions from verified institutional metadata to prevent erroneous historical claims.

The fundamental objective of this project is to develop and validate HeritageAI, an automated computer vision intelligence and digital preservation platform that accepts arbitrary digital imagery, applies rigorous aspect-ratio preserving preprocessing, performs multi-class Convolutional Neural Network inference across Warli, Madhubani, and Indian Textile categories, provides Grad-CAM attention visualizations, and securely persists catalog records through a verified review workflow.

1.2 Objectives

The specific technical and research objectives accomplished in this project are:

  1. Research-Grade Preprocessing: Design an automated image ingestion pipeline capable of validating file extensions (JPG, PNG, WebP), correcting EXIF orientation metadata, letterbox padding images to 224 × 224 × 3 RGB to prevent aspect-ratio skewing, and normalizing pixel intensities to [0.0, 1.0].
  2. Custom CNN Architecture: Implement a custom stacked Conv2D neural network with ReLU activations, 2 × 2 max-pooling, dense fully connected layers, 0.45 dropout regularization, and a Softmax classifier optimized via Adam ($lr = 0.0001$) under Sparse Categorical Crossentropy.
  3. Model Interpretability (Explainable AI): Integrate Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM) to extract gradient signals from the final convolutional layer, producing spatial heatmaps that illustrate regions of model attention.
  4. Enterprise Multi-Tier Architecture: Develop an enterprise backend using Java 21, Spring Boot 3, Spring Security with stateless JWT tokens, Spring Data JPA, and Flyway database migrations backed by PostgreSQL.
  5. Interactive Research Interface: Deliver a modern, accessible web application in React 18, Vite, TypeScript, and Tailwind CSS incorporating an editorial cultural palette (Deep Indigo, Terracotta, Ivory, Gold), an AI Pattern Analyzer, an Archival Catalog, and a Research Telemetry Dashboard.
  6. Curatorial Integrity & Review Workflow: Establish an expert verification workflow that explicitly separates AI inferences from human-reviewed provenance, while raising alerts whenever confidence scores fall below a configurable threshold (0.60).
1.3 Challenges

The primary engineering and algorithmic challenges encountered during development include:

  • Aspect Ratio Distortion: Standard computer vision pipelines indiscriminately resize rectangular artifacts into square dimensions, artificially distorting motifs. Implementing letterbox zero-padding resolved this issue.
  • Intra-Class Natural Variation: Handcrafted artworks exhibit vast textural variation across raw cloth, mud walls, and paper substrates.
  • Algorithmic Interpretability: Deep models act as opaque black boxes. Curation experts require visual justification for predictions before accepting automated attributions.

1.4 Literature Survey

Cultural pattern identification has progressively evolved from manual rule-based heuristics to modern deep convolutional architectures:

  • Handcrafted Feature Descriptors: Early research utilized Scale-Invariant Feature Transform (SIFT) and Histogram of Oriented Gradients (HOG) to extract corner and edge primitives. While partially effective for rigid geometries, these methods proved sensitive to substrate textures and non-uniform lighting conditions.
  • Standard Transfer Learning Backbones: General-purpose backbones such as AlexNet and ResNet-50 have demonstrated high accuracy in art historical classification. However, heavy deep residual networks exhibit substantial parameter counts, higher computational memory requirements, and elevated inference latency.
  • Proposed Lightweight Pipeline: The research paper foundation establishes that a customized stacked convolutional network with moderate depth (3 Conv2D blocks with filter progressions 32 → 64 → 128) achieves optimal discriminative capability for Indian motifs without the overhead of massive transfer backbones.

CHAPTER 2
SOFTWARE REQUIREMENT SPECIFICATION
2.1 Functional Requirements

The functional requirements define the explicit operational behaviors and interfaces implemented in HeritageAI:

  1. Authentication & Access Control: Secure user authentication using BCrypt salted password hashing and stateless JSON Web Tokens (JWT). Support for role-based authorization across ROLE_ADMIN, ROLE_RESEARCHER, and ROLE_ARCHIVIST.
  2. Image Preprocessing & Normalization: Automated verification of input file formats (.jpg, .jpeg, .png, .webp), EXIF orientation correction, zero-padded aspect-ratio preservation to 224 × 224 × 3, and floating-point intensity scaling [0.0, 1.0].
  3. Neural Classification Engine: Execution of CNN inference yielding Softmax confidence estimates across Warli, Madhubani, and Textile categories, accompanied by measured inference latencies.
  4. Low-Confidence Alert Trigger: Automatic activation of a visual warning indicator whenever the predicted category confidence score is below the 0.60 threshold.
  5. Explainable AI Visualization: Extraction of gradient activations from the final convolutional feature block and generation of visual Grad-CAM heatmap overlays.
  6. Preservation Catalog & Curatorial Review: Full archival cataloging storing image paths, provenance, and descriptions. Archival review workflow allowing experts to correct classifications without overwriting baseline AI predictions.
  7. Data Export & Audit Trail: On-demand export of catalog metadata to CSV format and persistent logging of system actions into an audit repository.
2.2 Non-functional Requirements
  • Performance & Latency: Image preprocessing and neural inference must complete within 300 ms on standard commodity CPUs.
  • Reliability & Fault Tolerance: The application must handle corrupted images, service disconnects, and uninitialized models gracefully without application failure.
  • Security: All passwords stored via BCrypt hashing; uploaded file storage uses randomized UUID naming to prevent path traversal exploits.

CHAPTER 4
SYSTEM IMPLEMENTATION
4.1 Module Description

4.1.1 Image Preprocessing Module:
The preprocessing module (ai-service/app/preprocessing.py) implements letterbox zero-padding. Rather than distorting motif geometry, images are scaled uniformly to fit within a 224 × 224 canvas, with remaining borders padded with black zero pixels. Pixel values are then divided by 255.0 to yield normalized floats in [0.0, 1.0].

4.1.2 Custom HeritageCNN Architecture:
The model architecture (ai-service/app/model.py) comprises stacked Conv2D feature extraction layers, max-pooling, dense fully connected layers, dropout regularization, and a 3-class Softmax layer:

Table 4.1: HeritageCNN Layer Parameter Configuration
Layer Group Layer Type Hyperparameters Output Shape Activation
Input InputLayer RGB Image (224, 224, 3) —
Block 1 Conv2D × 2 + MaxPool 32 filters, 3 × 3, Stride 1 (112, 112, 32) ReLU
Block 2 Conv2D × 2 + MaxPool 64 filters, 3 × 3, Stride 1 (56, 56, 64) ReLU
Block 3 Conv2D × 2 + MaxPool 128 filters, 3 × 3, Stride 1 (28, 28, 128) ReLU (target_conv_layer)
Dense Flatten + Dense 256 Units (256) ReLU
Regularization Dropout Rate = 0.45 (256) —
Output Dense 3 Units (Warli, Madhubani, Textile) (3) Softmax

4.1.3 Explainable AI (Grad-CAM Module):
Grad-CAM (ai-service/app/gradcam.py) accesses activations from target_conv_layer and computes positive gradients relative to the predicted class score. The rectified spatial map is normalized, upsampled to 224 × 224, and blended onto the input photograph to provide curatorial transparency.

4.1.4 Main Enterprise Backend Engine:
The Spring Boot backend encompasses:

  • SecurityConfig.java: Enforces stateless JWT validation and role-based route gating.
  • PredictionService.java: Coordinates file persistence, microservice dispatch, probability mapping, and relational database logging.
  • ArtifactService.java: Provides filtered search queries, curatorial reviews, and CSV catalog generation.

4.2 Tools, Language and RDBMS used
  • Java 21 & Spring Boot 3.2.3: Chosen for robust transactional integrity, strong type-safety, and enterprise microservice ecosystem.
  • Python 3.11 & FastAPI: Asynchronous high-throughput web server interfacing with TensorFlow C++ acceleration.
  • TensorFlow / Keras 2.15: Core framework for convolutional graph execution and automatic differentiation.
  • PostgreSQL 16: Industry-standard object-relational database ensuring ACID compliance and relational indexing.
  • React 18 & TypeScript: Single-page user interface ensuring responsive user experiences and declarative component hierarchies.
  • Jupyter Notebook Environment: Self-contained research pipeline (heritage_ai_research_pipeline.ipynb) supporting Google Colab cloud execution.

CHAPTER 6
CONCLUSION AND FUTURE ENHANCEMENTS
6.1 Conclusion

The HeritageAI platform successfully implements and operationalizes the research paper and patent application for automatic identification, classification, and digital preservation of Indian cultural patterns. By coupling an aspect-ratio preserving computer vision preprocessing pipeline with a tailored stacked Conv2D architecture, the system achieves research-grade classification across Warli, Madhubani, and Indian Textile motifs.

The integration of Grad-CAM explainability bridges the gap between deep learning and art historical curation by visually highlighting the spatial motif regions responsible for classification. Furthermore, the multi-tier architecture—combining a React/TypeScript interface, an enterprise Spring Boot REST API, a FastAPI AI microservice, and a PostgreSQL relational repository—ensures robust data provenance, prevents algorithmic overwriting of historical records, and provides cultural institutions with a turnkey preservation platform.

6.2 Future Enhancements
  1. Multi-Label Object Detection & Semantic Segmentation: Extend the architecture using YOLOv8 or Mask R-CNN to localize and segment individual motifs (e.g., distinguishing multiple Tarpa dancers and musical instruments) within large-scale composite murals.
  2. Expansion of Cultural Pattern Taxonomy: Broaden the classification taxonomy from three categories to encompass over twenty indigenous South Asian art and textile traditions, including Kalamkari, Pattachitra, Phad, Gond, and Tanjore paintings.
  3. Edge Quantization for Offline Field Archiving: Convert and quantize trained Keras models into TensorFlow Lite (TFLite) and ONNX formats to enable offline mobile inference on edge devices used by archivists in rural field sites lacking continuous internet connectivity.
